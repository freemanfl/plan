# Web Studio Action Map

> Purpose: actionable map derived from the Web Studio Master Plan.
> The Master Plan is the compass: it explains the business and why it should work.
> This document is the map: it turns that understanding into a rough sequence of work.
> It should stay simple for now and become more detailed only as the business model becomes clearer.

## Stage 1 — Prove the Core Production Idea

### Goal
Build a repeatable system that can take realistic small-business information and produce a decent first website without risking a real client.

### First test
Use an existing local business as a fake client.

Example:
- choose a restaurant, studio, barber, coach, photographer, etc.;
- collect only information publicly available about the business;
- role-play the client intake;
- feed the information through the planned production system;
- see what the system produces;
- record where AI succeeds, fails, or needs human help.

### Target
The automated/system-generated result does not need to be perfect.

A useful first milestone is a **4–7/10 website produced by the system**, which can then be elevated through human judgment, design, SEO, QA, and visual polish.

---

## Stage 2 — Build the Website Production Architecture

### Client intake
Design a simple intake structure, initially likely a Google Form.

The system must tolerate different levels of client preparation:
- strong existing website;
- weak or outdated website;
- good or bad photos;
- existing copy;
- scattered information;
- almost no prepared material.

Human normalization is allowed and expected initially.

### Input analysis
Create a process that can:
- gather client/public information;
- understand what exists;
- sort information into a hierarchy;
- identify missing material;
- evaluate images;
- decide what copy can be reused;
- generate missing copy where appropriate.

### Funnel system
Create a small number of reusable website funnels.

The basic conversion logic should be standardized, while allowing different business genres to use different structures where necessary.

### Layout system
Build reusable section layouts and variants that can accept:
- client information;
- images;
- generated/reused copy;
- funnel choice;
- business type.

The goal is controlled variety rather than designing every site from zero.

---

## Stage 3 — Build the Quality / Design Layers

The system-generated base site is then elevated in layers.

### Base quality
- responsive behavior;
- speed/performance;
- typography;
- spacing;
- navigation;
- accessibility basics;
- technical consistency.

### On-page and local SEO
Develop a repeatable process for:
- headings and structure;
- metadata;
- internal content structure;
- local-business information;
- schema where useful;
- search-engine-friendly technical basics.

Off-page SEO is a future capability, not part of the initial promise.

### Visual design
Build reusable rules for:
- colors;
- typography;
- imagery;
- spacing;
- visual hierarchy;
- components;
- backgrounds and decorative elements.

### Enrichment library
Collect reusable small details that make ordinary sections feel better:
- animation;
- transitions;
- image treatments;
- hover effects;
- lighting/gradient tricks;
- typography details;
- interaction patterns.

### Signature effect
Where appropriate, add one stronger modern visual element inspired by high-quality contemporary websites.

The intention is not to create a bespoke award site, but to give a practical small-business site a controlled higher-end touch.

---

## Stage 4 — Quality Assurance

Create a repeatable pre-launch QA layer.

Eventually it should cover:
- mobile/responsive behavior;
- broken links;
- forms;
- obvious content errors;
- visual consistency;
- performance;
- SEO basics;
- deployment;
- common technical failures.

AI should perform as much routine checking as practical, with human responsibility remaining at the final approval stage.

---

## Stage 5 — Define the Sellable Product

Once the production system can reliably create decent sites, define the commercial offer precisely.

### Core proposition
A good-looking, fast, functional small-business website plus ongoing responsibility for it.

The deeper sell is:
**the client has someone responsible for the website without hiring a full-time technical person or expensive agency.**

### Ongoing service
The recurring value should include a controlled amount of:
- hosting;
- maintenance;
- fixing website problems;
- information updates;
- image changes;
- service/price updates;
- other normal business-driven changes.

The service is not unlimited design work.

### Ownership
Clients should be able to leave and take the website.

Retention should come from convenience, responsibility, trust, and support—not lock-in.

---

## Stage 6 — Maintenance Automation

Design the support pipeline so simple client requests require very little developer attention.

### Target flow
1. Client sends a normal request by an easy channel such as email or messaging.
2. AI reads and classifies it.
3. The original client request is shown to the human approver.
4. AI finds the relevant repository/code/content.
5. AI prepares the proposed change.
6. The approver sees both the request and the proposed change.
7. Approve / reject / escalate.
8. Approved change is tested and deployed.

### Human roles
Initially:
- founder handles technical exceptions and system improvement;
- wife may assist with intake, normalization, and approval.

AI reduces repetitive work; it does not remove human accountability.

---

## Stage 7 — Prove Maintenance Economics

Before scaling, simulate many realistic maintenance requests.

Examples:
- change opening hours;
- replace photos;
- update a service;
- change prices;
- add temporary information;
- edit copy;
- fix a form;
- small layout correction.

Measure:
- AI success rate;
- average human attention;
- requests that require developer escalation;
- failure modes;
- whether the approval interface is understandable without deep technical knowledge.

The key business metric is **human attention per client**, not hosting cost.

---

## Stage 8 — Client Funnel and Sales System

Production alone does not create a business.

Design the full path:
**attention → trust → offer → qualification → purchase → intake**

### Initial market
Use Russian-speaking immigrants and entrepreneurs in Czechia, and potentially elsewhere in the EU, as an initial beachhead where language, shared experience, trust, and community relationships provide an advantage.

This is an initial sales advantage, not necessarily the permanent market limit.

### Client qualification
Prefer practical, cooperative, low-friction clients.

Reject or later offboard clients who:
- are aggressive or abusive;
- continually push outside scope;
- consume disproportionate support time;
- expect unlimited agency work from a low-cost subscription.

Client selection is part of protecting the economics.

### Proof
Eventually create:
- demo sites;
- portfolio/reference work;
- examples of improvement;
- a clear explanation of why the service is inexpensive without being disposable.

The production system should first become capable of creating this proof.

---

## Stage 9 — Pricing Experiments

Do not lock the model to a specific low number such as $20/month.

The initial price must balance two risks:
- too expensive → not enough clients to test the model;
- too cheap → too much demand, poor client selection, low perceived value, and overload while the system is immature.

Early pricing should intentionally tolerate some rejection.

The initial objective is:
**find a price high enough to protect workload and perceived value, while still slowly acquiring enough clients to learn.**

Pricing should be tuned from reality.

---

## Stage 10 — First Real Clients

Only after the fake-client production tests and basic maintenance system are good enough should the studio begin taking real clients.

The first real milestone is not 100 clients.

It is something closer to:
- acquire a few strangers;
- produce their sites successfully;
- keep them happy;
- measure support;
- learn where the offer is misunderstood;
- improve the system.

Getting roughly 10 real paying clients over time would already provide meaningful evidence about acquisition, retention, support load, and pricing.

---

## Stage 11 — Legal and Operational Foundation

Before meaningful commercial scale, define:
- contracts / terms;
- GDPR and privacy;
- cookies where relevant;
- domain ownership;
- hosting responsibility;
- cancellation;
- ownership/IP;
- client-provided images/content;
- liability boundaries;
- payment handling.

This should be sufficient and practical rather than over-engineered.

---

## Stage 12 — Controlled Scale

Scale only after the economics are visible.

Track:
- recurring revenue;
- churn;
- average human support time;
- AI/tool cost;
- hosting/infrastructure cost;
- acquisition effort;
- number of escalations;
- client quality.

The business goal is not maximum scale or an IPO.

The goal is a **safe, stable, controlled recurring family income**.

The system can grow slowly, preserving good clients and rejecting growth that damages the workload or quality of life.
