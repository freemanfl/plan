# Web Studio Sales & Client Funnel

## Purpose

This document stores the acquisition, qualification, sales, and conversion system.

The production system is only useful if strangers can be convinced to buy it.

---

## 1. Core Sales Problem

Client acquisition is likely harder than building the sites.

The sales system needs to answer:
- who needs this;
- what triggers the need;
- where those people can be reached;
- why they should trust us;
- why they should buy this instead of DIY/freelancer/agency/no website;
- what price feels credible;
- how to reduce risk before purchase.

---

## 2. Initial Beachhead Market

Strong initial niche:
**Russian-speaking immigrants / entrepreneurs in Czechia, potentially wider EU later.**

Advantages:
- shared language;
- easier trust/communication;
- founder understands immigrant context;
- avoids relying immediately on strong Czech-language sales;
- community referrals may be stronger.

This is a beachhead, not necessarily the permanent market ceiling.

---

## 3. Client Funnel

Target logic:

**Attention → Trust → Offer → Proof → Qualification → Prototype / Risk Reduction → Purchase → Intake → Build → Ongoing Relationship**

---

## 4. Acquisition Research

Research where small-business website demand naturally appears.

Questions:
- Who buys a first website?
- Who replaces a weak one?
- What business events trigger the purchase?
- Where do new entrepreneurs congregate online/offline?
- Which administrative/legal/business processes expose predictable need?
- Which local communities contain many practical small-business owners?
- Where can outreach be highly targeted rather than broad advertising?

---

## 5. Prototype-First Selling

Build 76's prototype mechanic is a strong idea worth testing.

Potential flow:
1. identify qualified business;
2. create fast homepage concept/prototype;
3. show concrete result;
4. explain one simple offer;
5. convert or walk away.

Why it may work:
- reduces buyer uncertainty;
- proves design visually;
- makes the sales conversation concrete;
- especially useful because design is the most visible differentiator.

Risk:
- wasted work on non-buyers.

Therefore prototype production must become very fast and standardized.

---

## 6. Pricing Philosophy

Do not anchor to an artificially low price just to seem easy to buy.

Early-stage logic:
- immature system + too many cheap clients is dangerous;
- price should be high enough to keep growth slow and manageable;
- rejection is acceptable while learning;
- easier to lower a price than raise a large base of underpriced legacy clients.

The exact price is still experimental.

---

## 7. Qualification

Qualify for:
- reasonable expectations;
- normal communication style;
- scope fit;
- business legitimacy;
- likely maintenance burden;
- willingness to use the standard system.

Reject/offboard clients who are:
- abusive/aggressive;
- chronic scope-pushers;
- excessively high-maintenance;
- expecting unlimited custom design/marketing at productized pricing.

---

## 8. Early Validation Goal

Do not optimize for 100 clients immediately.

Even roughly **10 paying strangers in year one** would be meaningful proof that:
- the offer is understandable;
- strangers trust it;
- production works;
- maintenance is survivable;
- referrals/retention can begin.

---

## 9. Proof Assets to Build

Eventually:
- own studio landing page;
- prototype examples;
- before/after redesign examples;
- performance/quality proof;
- testimonials;
- live client sites;
- simple explanation of process;
- clear maintenance/responsibility promise.

The production system should eventually help generate its own proof assets.
