# Web Studio Offer & Service Scope

## Purpose

This document defines what the client is actually buying, what is included, what is excluded, and where custom work begins.

The current direction is intentionally **one simple offer**, not multiple artificial plans.

---

## 1. Core Offer

A good-looking, modern, fast, technically solid small-business website or landing page with ongoing responsibility attached to it.

The physical product is a website.

The deeper product is **peace of mind**:
- the website exists;
- it looks professional;
- it works;
- it stays maintained;
- ordinary changes can be requested without technical knowledge;
- the business owner does not need to manage hosting, code, plugins, updates, or routine web work.

Useful shorthand:

**You get a good website, and someone remains responsible for it.**

---

## 2. One Offer, One Price

Current preference:
- one core subscription/offer;
- one clearly bounded service;
- no tier structure where more money means substantially more founder attention;
- anything materially outside scope is quoted separately.

Core principle:

**Revenue should scale faster than human attention.**

No tier should intentionally sell substantially more founder time.

---

## 3. Included Core Services

Likely core inclusions:
- website design;
- website build;
- hosting;
- maintenance;
- normal business-driven content updates;
- on-page SEO;
- basic local SEO foundations;
- forms/lead capture;
- basic analytics;
- simple integrations where appropriate;
- performance and technical QA;
- responsive/mobile support;
- tasteful animation and visual polish.

Exact final wording and limits still need to be tested commercially.

---

## 4. Maintenance Scope

Maintenance is not unlimited redesign.

Normal included business-driven changes can include:
- replace images;
- update business information;
- change opening hours;
- change prices;
- update services;
- replace/edit copy;
- add a normal piece of content;
- update banners/notices;
- fix ordinary website issues.

Not automatically included:
- complete redesigns;
- repeated subjective style experimentation;
- large new features;
- custom backend systems;
- complex integrations;
- major new page systems;
- full marketing campaigns;
- heavy manual content production.

Those become separate custom work if accepted at all.

---

## 5. Ownership / Exit

Current philosophy:
- avoid coercive lock-in;
- clients should be able to leave/take the site under fair terms;
- the business should retain clients because maintenance/responsibility is useful, not because departure is technically painful.

It is acceptable if a client subscribes for a relatively short period and leaves feeling they received an unusually good deal, provided the economics remain viable.

---

## 6. SEO Positioning

Included initially:
- good on-page SEO;
- basic local SEO foundations;
- technical SEO basics.

Not initially promised:
- guaranteed rankings;
- guaranteed traffic;
- serious backlink campaigns;
- broad off-page SEO;
- full digital marketing agency services.

---

## 7. Ideal Client

Best fit:
- tiny/small business owners;
- practical people;
- businesses that want a credible site but do not want to manage it themselves;
- photographers, dancers, coaches, barbers, restaurants, studios, trades, local service businesses, etc.

Poor fit:
- aggressive/abusive clients;
- chronic scope-pushers;
- clients expecting unlimited custom design;
- clients requiring enterprise systems at small-business pricing;
- clients whose support demands destroy unit economics.

Client filtering is part of the business model.

---

## 8. Risk Reversal / Prototype Idea

Build 76's free homepage prototype is a strong sales mechanism worth considering.

Possible flow:
**lead → prototype/homepage concept → client sees direction → purchase → full build**

Why it is powerful:
- removes fear of paying for something unseen;
- proves visual competence before commitment;
- turns the sales conversation into evaluation of a concrete result.

Need to test whether this can be produced cheaply enough to avoid wasting too much time on non-buyers.
